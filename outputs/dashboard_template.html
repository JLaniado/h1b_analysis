Reading this responsibly

  • PERM ≠ "will sponsor an outside hire." ~86% of PERM filings are green-card conversions for someone already employed there, not offers to outside candidates — and that share is even higher among the large repeat filers this dashboard covers (the smaller, one-off filers not shown here skew more external). Check "Show only external hires" to see just that minority.
  • "First-time hire" (H-1B) = a new position, or an H-1B transfer in from another employer, or a new concurrent position — all three mean the worker is joining this employer for the first time. It excludes extensions and petition amendments for someone already there. Check "Show only first-time hires" to filter to just those.
  • Wage level is a role proxy, not an employer's actual bar. Two employers filing "Level II" for the same title can still have very different real hiring bars.
  • MBA-relevance tiers are a heuristic (SOC code + title matching), not a real minimum-degree field, and this explorer only covers occupations in the core/adjacent tiers — technical titles like "Data Scientists" or "Software Developers" are excluded here even though a business-analytics or quant background might realistically target them. If that's your profile, treat this tool as one input, not the full picture, and cross-check DOL's LCA/PERM disclosure data directly for those titles.
  • Employer names are canonicalized and known multi-subsidiary brands are rolled up (e.g. Amazon's, Goldman Sachs's, and Deloitte's legal entities all combine into one row) — but this covers a manually-reviewed list of ~20 major employers, not every subsidiary in the dataset.
  • Averages shown are means of annualized wage, not medians (only sums/counts are pre-aggregated for performance) — treat them as directional.
